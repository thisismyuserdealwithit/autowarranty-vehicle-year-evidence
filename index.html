One more hour or a higher hourly allowance?

A repair takes eight hours. The administrator agrees to pay for six at up to $125 an hour. The shop asks for a change. Would the driver save more if the administrator approved a seventh hour or raised the hourly allowance to $150?

Across 50 modeled metro shop rates, one more hour saves more in 24 metros. The higher allowance saves more in 26. The choice comes within eight cents in Grand Rapids, Michigan.

Try the Grand Rapids case

The modeled shop rate is $145.82. The administrator accepts all $600 in parts and applies one $100 deductible. Assume the same repair, with no tax, fees or further payment limit. Work out the reduction in the driver’s bill under each change.

Check the arithmetic

The seventh hour saves $125. Raising the allowance lets the administrator pay the shop’s $145.82 rate for six hours. That adds $20.82 per hour, or $124.92. The seventh hour saves eight cents more.

What if the shop charged $145.84?

Six times $20.84 is $125.04. The higher rate would now save four cents more than the additional hour. At prices stated in whole cents, $145.84 is the first shop rate above $125 where the higher allowance wins.
